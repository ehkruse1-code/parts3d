How it was built

  1. Recover the camerasVGGT, a 3-D vision model running locally, worked out where each photo was taken from and produced a rough 3-D point cloud of the part.
  2. Find the scale without measuringThe pulley is a standard 6-rib serpentine pulley, whose grooves are 3.56 mm apart. Measuring that spacing in the side photo (18 px) sets the size of everything else.
  3. Design it as real CADThe part is built from proper engineering shapes: turned housings, a grooved pulley, round mounting pillars, 48 cooling fins, and the vent windows traced from the photos and cut through the castings.
  4. Score it against the photos, every timeEach change is rendered from all three cameras and compared with the photos — outlines, edges, and the depth of every surface. A change stays only if the score improves.

What to keep in mind

Measured where it was seen

Everything visible in at least one photo is measured from it. The top, the bottom and the unphotographed side are reasonable fill-ins.

Materials are generic

Cast aluminium, black-coated steel, brass, copper and plastic are standard material settings, not colours taken from the photos.

One part, so far

This alternator was designed by hand with the measuring tools. An automatic version that takes any three photos is in progress.
